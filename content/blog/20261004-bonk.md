description: A simple time-waster built in an afternoon.

---

I've been feeling quite miserable lately. I find myself in an unfortunate situation where generative AI has stormed the shores of all things programming, and I'm feeling a little bit led astray. I've spent more than half of my life being deeply in love with creating and designing websites, learning new technologies and frameworks in order to hone my skills... and yet I can't help but feel a little bit like it's been for nothing. I have many thoughts and opinions about how quickly generative AI has not only evolved, but also been adopted and integrated into every online service under the sun, but this isn't really the space for that.

Before today, I hadn't written any code in a **long** time - at least a few months. After finishing my diploma, my focus immediately went to the job search... however my efforts have not yet yielded any fruit (but let's not go there). I desperately needed a small project that I could complete in an afternoon, something that I could get done just to make sure I was still familiar with the basics. And something at least a *little* bit fun.

So I made [Bonk](https://bonk.rackodo.dev).

![A simple image with a white background, a primitive hammer made of a red rectangle as a handle and a blue rectangle as the head on the left, with the text "Bonk - A thing by Rackodo" on the right.](/images/20261004-bonk-banner.png)

You use your mouse to control a hammer and click to hit an orange target to increase your score. That's all there is to it. The goal wasn't to make something engaging or distinctly interesting, or something that had any kind of use. It only served as an exercise to see how quickly I could put together an interactive experience that could be used to kill maybe 1 or 2 minutes of spare time. Easy!

It doesn't use anything other than simple HTML, CSS and Javascript. It's *intentionally* light on resources - I didn't want to spend time making resources like images or animations, so I relied on basic CSS to create a simple hammer, a target and something for it to sit on. I've tried loading it using simulated 3G speeds, and it still loads in less than 5 seconds. On the other hand, I also didn't spend any time implementing responsiveness. I relied on fixed sizes and positions, and decided to just block the page entirely on mobile screens because I didn't want to come up with a control system for touchscreens. But I don't think mobile users will be missing out on much.

All this to say, it felt really good to make something again. I could see myself potentially expanding on this in the future, since the code was relatively simple enough, and I can already think of one or two ways to improve upon it to add more features. Either way, I'm just happy to know that I managed to get this done and in a state that feels somewhat acceptable.

You can check out the source code [here](https://github.com/rackodo/bonk), if you're interested in tinkering with it on your own! Otherwise, hope you have a good day :)

-B.
